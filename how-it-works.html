How it works

Plans that hold up, no matter what happens.

Coherence uses risk-aware optimization to plan your plant's operations and energy investments against millions of possible futures, not a single forecast. You get decisions that stay sound when prices spike, markets shift or the process surprises you.

01 / Risk-aware optimization

Optimal on paper isn't good enough.

Most energy plans are tuned to a single forecast. When reality differs, and it always does, the savings shrink or turn into losses. We plan against the full range of what could happen and protect you against the worst cases.

  • Robust, not fragile. We give up a little on the best case to take the nasty surprises off the table.

  • One method for operations and investments. Day-ahead schedules and ten-year equipment decisions are judged the same way.

  • Your risk tolerance, your call. Decide how conservative the plan should be, and see exactly what that costs or saves.

04 / Three kinds of uncertainty

What a forecast can't tell you, and how we plan for it anyway.

Plants face three very different kinds of uncertainty. Each one breaks a single-forecast plan in its own way, and each one needs its own answer.

Worked example

A seawater reverse-osmosis desalination plant with several RO trains, a product-water storage tank, and a time-of-use tariff with demand charges. Power is one of its largest operating costs. (Hypothetical plant, used for illustration.)

  1. 01 / Short-term

    Price spikes and disruptions

    Some events are hard to forecast, and some are impossible to forecast with the data available.

    In the example

    A heat wave sends real-time power prices to ten times normal for three hours on a weekday afternoon. A week later, a high-pressure pump trips during the evening ramp. Neither shows up in any forecast. A plan tuned to the forecast was filling the product tank that afternoon, so it was caught running every train at the worst possible moment.

    How Coherence plans for it

    • Keep tank level and spare capacity in reserve, sized to how likely and how costly spikes and trips really are across millions of scenarios.
    • Pre-plan the response: which trains drop first, for how long, and still meet delivery.
    • Turn spikes into savings instead of bill shocks.
  2. 02 / Long-term

    Market trends and recurring value

    Installing a new process line or a battery rests on key assumptions about the market and how much value will recur, year after year.

    In the example

    The plant is weighing a battery to cut demand charges. Its value depends on what the next rate case does to demand charges, whether demand-response programs keep paying, how much new solar flattens midday prices, and how fast water demand grows. A battery sized to last year's tariff can look great on paper and be stranded three years later.

    How Coherence plans for it

    • Evaluate every option, from battery sizes to an extra RO train to doing nothing, across thousands of market futures, together with how the plant would operate around it.
    • Pick the option whose payback holds in most futures, not just the expected one. Often that's a smaller battery paired with operational flexibility.
    • Show the full range of returns, so your capital request survives finance's toughest questions.
  3. 03 / Execution

    Running a plant is an art

    Keeping a facility stable with careful chemistry sometimes behaves differently than you expect.

    In the example

    Dropping from five RO trains to three at 4 pm changes feed pressure, recovery, and how antiscalant and pretreatment chemistry behave. Cycling membranes too often can speed up scaling and fouling, and pretreatment can take hours to settle. A schedule that looks fine in a spreadsheet can push a water-quality parameter toward its limit.

    How Coherence plans for it

    • Model ramp rates, minimum run times and start/stop limits explicitly.
    • Keep a margin from chemistry and quality limits that reflects how uncertain the plant's response really is.
    • Learn from your SCADA data how your plant actually responds, and tighten margins only as confidence grows. Your operators stay in the loop.
